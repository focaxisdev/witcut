# Art direction: make attention earn its motion

Read for storyboarding, composition, pacing, or an aesthetically weak cut.

## Behavior becomes the story

Before writing narration or captions, make a small table:

| Mode | Input | User action | Automatic result | Remaining manual work | Relationship to other modes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Observed mode | Actual input | Actual control | Verified response | Explicit adjustment | Alternative / sequential / composable |

A cut can remove waiting but must not turn a manual adjustment into automation. If a claim depends on an intermediate action, show it or clearly explain the omission. Do not imply that three alternative modes are three required steps.

Choose one reason the viewer should care. Organize the film around useful outcomes rather than an exhaustive control inventory. For a new visual direction, compare a few genuinely different approaches when helpful; do not force a fixed number of concepts for a small revision.

## One shot, one job

For each shot record:

- What the viewer should notice or understand.
- What must remain visible to make that information meaningful.
- Source state/range and any editorial transformation.
- Needed reading/settling time.
- Why this transition is better than a simple cut.

Inspect styleframes at delivery and thumbnail size, then test motion. A beautiful still frame is not evidence that its transition works.

## Motivated zoom

Ask: What becomes newly legible? Why now? Which spatial relationship must survive? What does the settled frame prove?

- **Object emphasis:** prefer a clear centered or optically balanced treatment when the object itself is the emphasis; honor a justified alternate composition. Account for visual weight rather than blindly centering a bounding box.
- **Relationship emphasis:** compose the object with its result, target, or surroundings; do not center one while accidentally severing the relationship.
- **No information gain:** remove the zoom or use a quieter hold.

Centering, thirds, and golden-ratio placements are tools, not universal acceptance equations. Choose a composition supported by the subject. Avoid detached or rearranged native controls that appear to be the real interface.

Check the beginning, entire eased trajectory, and ending for clipped controls, abrupt focal changes, unreadable labels, accidental edges, or loss of context. Uniform scaling preserves geometry. Full-bleed, inset, vertical, and split layouts are valid when intentional and appropriate to the brief.

When no useful continuous path exists, use a **same-state insert**: show a clearer crop of the same recorded result, then return to context if needed. Do not dissolve between different input/result states to conceal missing operations. A held result is editorial presentation, not evidence of live animation.

## Pace that carries meaning

Leave enough time to recognize the action and its result. If a duration limit conflicts with necessary proof, surface the tradeoff; when the limit is relaxed, stop optimizing against the obsolete limit.

For an aerial-feeling opening, capture a continuous, deliberate camera move with a settle. Padding a still does not create that effect. Remove redundant endings only if the removed view adds no useful understanding: a detail followed by a context view may be justified.

Use restrained easing and a coherent motion vocabulary. Refined does not mean slow everywhere. Branding and outros should follow the actual visual system and destination; a solid-color card is one option, not a requirement.

## Bounded refinement

Review against purpose: can a first-time viewer identify the operation and understand its result? Name the defect and its impact. Preserve already-approved behavior and brand treatment when correcting a local composition. Apply the [shared loop contract](collaboration.md); treat low-benefit polish separately from blockers, especially after owner acceptance.
