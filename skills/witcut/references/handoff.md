# Handoff: one candidate, honest recovery

Read for final delivery, publication, or production backups.

## Freeze and identify

Keep approved sources and masters intact. Render revisions to separate output locations and identify rejected or failed exports so they cannot be mistaken for the final candidate. Use the selected candidate's version, bytes, and checksum in delivery and QA records when available.

A new resolution or audio export is a new artifact requiring relevant checks, even if the edit is unchanged. Disclose mixed-source upscaling. Do not add an approval ritual for already-authorized work, but do not infer permission to alter a frozen cut or publish it from permission to inspect or prepare it.

## Delivery is distinct from preparation

Report an upload or publication as complete only after verifying the saved artifact or destination state. Verify the authorized account/channel/repository rather than selecting a similar name. Keep private metadata out of public descriptions and packages. If access is unavailable, deliver a prepared artifact and explicitly leave publication pending.

Record media rights and required attribution separately from technical platform checks. Avoid promising clickable links, processing completion, or platform capabilities without checking the actual destination when needed. Hosting a finished video does not authorize changing the associated website.

## Choose the backup scope

- **Design-only:** authorized scripts/timeline/configuration/metadata and permitted assets, with a list of externally required media.
- **Self-contained:** all necessary sources and dependencies that can legally and practically be included.

Do not call the first the second. A finished MP4 is not a replacement for original takes, and recapture from a changing product cannot guarantee frame-identical recovery.

Include, as applicable:

- Editable shot plan/timeline and scripts actually used.
- Source ranges and capture/action timing records.
- Relative path configuration, runtime/dependency versions, fonts and asset provenance.
- QA records naming the exact approved artifact.
- A manifest of included files: relative path, bytes, SHA-256.
- A separate dependency inventory for omitted media: purpose, expected location/configuration, dimensions/timing where relevant, and retrieval requirements.
- A restore procedure with output sent to a fresh directory, not over the approved work.

Check that documented filenames, environment-variable names, and dependencies match the actual package. Avoid copying entire workspaces, secrets, private profiles, internal account identifiers, or unrelated history.

## Report the verification level actually reached

1. Package assembled and structure inspected.
2. Saved/uploaded package retrieved and integrity checked.
3. Dependencies and a source-decoding/render smoke test exercised in a stated environment.
4. End-to-end restoration completed in a clean environment and compared against defined expectations.

Hash equality proves integrity, not that a renderer starts. A local smoke test is not a clean-machine restoration. Record missing dependencies and platform limitations without hiding them behind “backup complete.” If access ends, leave the checkpoint, remaining work, and required resources; do not promise continued jobs on a disconnected computer.
