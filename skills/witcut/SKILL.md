# Witcut

Make the viewer understand a real product through purposeful filmmaking. This is a workflow, not an editing engine: use available, authorized tools and state which actions or observations are unavailable.

## Enter at the requested stage

Do not repeat the entire production process for a narrow review. Read only the relevant reference:

- Structured audiovisual script, shot plan, or podcast-to-video adaptation: [planning core](references/planning.md). Generate all three planning outputs from the same input; label unverified or intended states and record missing materials.
- Story, framing, rhythm, or weak visual direction: [art direction](references/art-direction.md).
- New recording, tiny UI, resolution, or motion issues: [capture](references/capture.md).
- Encoded draft, playback issue, or review-only request: [QA](references/qa.md).
- Approved cut, upload, backup, or restoration: [handoff](references/handoff.md).
- Model/reasoning selection, delegation, and bounded revision loops: [collaboration](references/collaboration.md). Apply the same loop contract at planning, source, execution, review, and audience-goal levels.
- Concrete failure patterns: [production lessons](examples/production-lessons.md).

For the bounded original-media execution test, use the [synthetic pilot](references/render-fixture.md); its successful render is not production acceptance.

## 1. Establish the current brief

Record the audience, one viewer takeaway, product/source access, destination, brand assets, duration constraints, audio, delivery dimensions, and permitted actions. Separate requirements from preferences and unknowns. Read the latest revision of the brief; an old duration limit must not override a later relaxation.

Identify the selected candidate, approved elements, read-only/frozen boundaries, and whether delivery or publication is authorized. Reuse existing authorization within its scope; ask only for missing information that materially changes the work. Do not modify the product or use a disconnected computer merely to ease filming.

## 2. Verify behavior before story

Observe each relevant mode's input, user action, system response, and remaining manual steps. Determine whether modes are alternatives, sequential stages, or composable tools. If access is missing, label the model unverified and do not invent a demonstration.

Build the story from authentic action to visible consequence. Editorial crops, overlays, time compression, and held frames must not imply a product capability or causal relationship that was not observed. Distinguish editorial graphics from native UI.

## 3. Pilot before expensive production

Define each shot's purpose, required context, source range, framing, reading/settling time, and transition. Test a representative short excerpt at the intended delivery size. Choose aspect ratio, browser zoom, capture resolution, duration, and ending from the brief—not a previous project's constants.

Use canonical brand assets and record their provenance. Check rendered fonts and geometry; silently substituting a font can change a lockup. Preserve attribution required by source material.

## 4. Capture and edit for comprehension

Preserve authentic source material and operation timestamps. Separate source capture cadence from output fps. Keep inputs, mode switches, manual adjustments, and results sufficient to support the claim.

For each zoom, explain the attention shift and verify the starting composition, intermediate path, and settled frame. Remove decorative movement that hides context. Consider a same-state result insert when a continuous crop cannot remain legible; never bridge incompatible states to fabricate continuity.

Let meaningful action and readable outcomes determine timing within the current brief. Extend real motion when it benefits the opening; repeating a still frame does not create an aerial move.

## 5. Review with bounded evidence

Review the actual encoded candidate, not just source canvases or contact sheets. Track technical readiness, narrative/art findings, and owner acceptance separately. Audio presence, nonzero signal, listening, output-device behavior, and rights are separate observations.

Record timestamped findings against an identified file/version, with impact and the smallest useful correction. Use an independent reviewer when consequential ambiguity warrants it; do not require one for every minor revision. Never substitute a perfect score for evidence.

Use the [shared loop contract](references/collaboration.md) to repair and stop. The top-level check remains viewer understanding of the current brief; passing every delegated task does not establish it. A read-only review produces findings only. A frozen cut stays unchanged unless modification is newly authorized.

## 6. Deliver the exact approved work

Identify the final candidate unambiguously and keep rejected outputs separate. Report what was inspected, what remains untested, and what has actually been delivered. Publishing and changing an approved cut are distinct actions; do not infer one from permission for the other.

Package the authorized sources with relative references, dependency versions, provenance, and a manifest. Distinguish design-only from self-contained backups and checksum integrity from end-to-end restoration. Leave a usable checkpoint if access ends; do not promise work on an unavailable machine.
